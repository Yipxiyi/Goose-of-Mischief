Use the uploaded image as the sole scene source. Preserve its composition, camera, subject relationships, and scene identity while reconstructing the entire image as a strongly recognizable pastoral low-poly storybook world. Integrate exactly one white goose performing one harmless act of mischief.

The result should clearly evoke the broad visual language associated with *Untitled Goose Game*: simplified low-poly forms, matte flat-painted materials, restrained pastoral colors, clean silhouettes, soft ambient daylight, faceless human figures, low-detail surfaces, and dry physical comedy.

Do not reproduce any commercial game's exact map, character mesh, level layout, UI, task list, logo, title treatment, or identifiable screenshot.

## Method

Use:

**DECONSTRUCT → SELECTIVE PRESERVATION → STYLE RECONSTRUCTION → GOOSE INTEGRATION → QUALITY GATE**

Complete the analysis internally and do not expose it unless requested.

### 1. Deconstruct

Identify three to six decisive visual facts:

- primary and secondary subjects
- relative scale
- dominant horizontal, vertical, or diagonal axes
- foreground, middle-ground, and background layers
- overlap and occlusion
- important intervals and negative space
- light direction and shadow softness
- key source colors and accents
- protected faces, pets, labels, screens, text, or focal objects

### 2. Selective Preservation

Preserve:

- original aspect ratio, crop, and camera angle
- source perspective and camera height
- subject count, position, action, and relative scale
- depth order and occlusion
- key identity colors and features
- scene topology and object relationships
- the original focal hierarchy

Simplify only photographic texture, micro-detail, surface noise, tiny decoration, and nonessential background clutter.

### 3. Style Reconstruction

Reconstruct every subject as simplified low-poly storybook geometry.

Use:

- object-level low-poly or softly faceted forms
- slightly chunky proportions
- broad clean color planes
- matte chalky hand-painted surfaces
- clear thumbnail-readable silhouettes
- minimal surface detail
- vegetation as clustered masses rather than individual leaves
- architecture and props reduced to simple readable volumes
- broad ambient daylight and short soft contact shadows

The style must be strong and immediately visible. It must not remain a lightly filtered photograph.

## Critical Anti-Filter Rule

Do not apply a uniform triangular mosaic across the photograph.

Avoid:

- stained-glass polygon filters
- crystalline or origami fragmentation
- identical triangle density on every surface
- realistic photography underneath transparent polygon texture
- generic vector posterization, watercolor, painterly, or editorial styling replacing the required look

Low-poly character must come from simplified object forms, silhouettes, materials, proportions, and lighting—not from pixel-level triangles.

## Color and Lighting

Extract important source colors, then harmonize them into a restrained pastoral storybook palette.

Preferred families:

- warm off-white and cream
- sage, moss, and soft grass green
- stone beige
- pale gray-blue
- weathered wood brown
- muted terracotta
- small brick-red, mustard, or dusty-orange accents

Preserve important clothing colors, pet markings, product colors, and environmental identity.

Do not replace every setting with a generic British village. Indoor, urban, product, food, and modern-device scenes must retain their original identity while adopting the required rendering language.

Use soft overcast, morning, or calm afternoon illumination, low-to-medium contrast, short soft-edged contact shadows, and little specular shine.

Avoid cinematic rim light, hard spotlighting, volumetric beams, heavy depth of field, bloom, and glossy 3D realism.

## Goose Design

Insert exactly one goose by default.

The goose should have:

- a clean simplified white body
- a long curved neck
- a flat orange beak
- orange webbed feet
- one tiny dark eye when visible
- matte flat-painted surfaces
- a clear readable silhouette
- plausible adult domestic-goose proportions

Avoid realistic feathers, toy scale, mascot proportions, giant-monster scale, human hands, teeth, clothes, eyelashes, or exaggerated human expressions.

## Physical Scale Rule

Do not size the goose using a percentage of the image dimensions.

Image-space size is not physical scale.

Before placing the goose, choose:

1. its depth plane
2. its support surface
3. at least two nearby real-world scale anchors
4. whether a full body physically fits
5. its occlusion plan

Useful anchors include:

- cats, dogs, or people
- chair seats, chair legs, and table height
- glasses, cups, bottles, plates, and containers
- doors, cabinets, appliances, and shelves
- floor tiles, steps, curbs, and path width
- shoes, hands, and bags

Treat the goose as a real medium-sized adult domestic bird—not a toy, icon, or mascot.

### Support-Surface Rule

A fully visible goose may appear only when the selected surface has enough plausible footprint for its body and feet.

If a full body cannot fit naturally:

- place it on a farther valid depth plane
- partially hide it behind furniture or architecture
- show only its head and neck entering from behind an object
- crop part of the body naturally at the image edge
- use a doorway, chair gap, cabinet gap, path, or background opening

Never shrink the goose into a toy merely to fit a small space.

Never enlarge it merely to make the joke obvious.

### Visibility Without Scale Cheating

Choose obvious, subtle, or hidden visibility based on the composition.

Control visibility through:

- placement on or away from the focal path
- silhouette contrast
- neck direction and gesture
- interaction with an existing object
- partial occlusion
- foreground, middle-ground, or background depth
- use of negative space

Do not use unrealistic scale to control salience.

### Perspective and Contact

The goose must match:

- source perspective and vanishing direction
- camera height
- support-surface angle
- selected depth plane
- light direction and color temperature
- shadow softness
- depth-of-field treatment
- overlap and occlusion order

Visible feet must contact the support surface and cast an appropriate short soft shadow.

## Mischief Design

Prefer an existing secondary object from the source image.

Possible actions:

- tugging a cable, strap, ribbon, scarf, shoelace, hose, or cloth edge
- carrying away a sock, glove, key, spoon, brush, toy, paper, flower, snack, or small tool
- nudging a cup, basket, ball, shoe, cushion, sign, or small container
- rearranging a tidy row or display
- peeking from behind an object while reaching for something nearby
- blocking a doorway or path
- lightly photobombing from a plausible depth layer

The prank must be harmless, simple, scene-aware, physically readable, local, and understandable without captions.

Do not invent a new prop when an existing object can support the joke.

## Subject Protection

Do not cover or substantially alter:

- faces and essential gestures
- the primary pet or animal
- the main product, artwork, dish, or food subject
- important labels, text, screens, or UI
- landmarks and scene-defining objects

When a cat, dog, or other pet is present:

- preserve it as the primary animal
- use the pet and nearby furniture as scale anchors
- make the goose a secondary intruder
- do not arbitrarily enlarge the goose for emphasis
- avoid aggression, fear, or harm

## Quality Gate

### Composition

- Is the source immediately recognizable?
- Are crop, camera, perspective, and subject relationships preserved?
- Are primary subject positions, actions, and identity cues intact?

### Style

- Does the image immediately read as a pastoral low-poly storybook world?
- Are objects rebuilt as simplified forms rather than filtered?
- Are surfaces matte and flat-painted?
- Is lighting soft and non-cinematic?
- Has photographic micro-detail been removed?
- Is the image free of uniform triangle-mosaic artifacts?

### Goose

- Is there exactly one goose?
- Were at least two scale anchors used?
- Is its adult physical scale plausible at the selected depth?
- Can the support surface actually fit its body?
- Does it match perspective, light, shadow, and occlusion?
- Is visibility achieved without scale cheating?
- Does it look native to the scene rather than pasted on?

### Mandatory Regeneration Conditions

Regenerate once if:

- the result still looks substantially photographic
- the image becomes a triangular polygon filter
- the pastoral low-poly storybook style is weak
- composition or camera changes
- the goose is too large, too small, floating, or unsupported
- the goose appears pasted on
- the prank is unrelated or unreadable
- an important subject, label, or text region is obscured

By default, return only the completed edited image. Do not expose lengthy analysis or the full prompt unless requested.
